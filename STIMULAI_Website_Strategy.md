## 1. Brand Narrative

STIMULAI exists at a threshold most companies never reach — the point where engineering stops being a discipline and becomes an obsession.

The company was not founded to sell products. It was founded to solve a problem that most of the world doesn't yet know exists: the distance between what the human body is capable of and what the technology surrounding it allows it to achieve. That gap is the mission. Closing it is the company.

The bicycle is not the point. The bicycle is proof. It is the first demonstration that STIMULAI's approach — combining embedded intelligence, advanced materials, and systems-level thinking about human performance — produces machines of a fundamentally different character. Machines that don't augment performance through brute force or gimmick, but through precision understanding of the athlete-machine interface.

This is the narrative the website must carry: **a company at the beginning of something enormous**, building quietly, methodically, without compromise, with the kind of engineering conviction that doesn't need to shout. The work speaks. The technology speaks. The website is not a sales tool — it is a declaration.

The tone is **McLaren Applied meets Specialized S-Works** — technically rigorous, aesthetically severe, emotionally charged. Not cold. Not clinical. Obsessive.

Every pixel, every line of copy, every animation must reinforce a single truth: **STIMULAI is not a product company. It is a performance engineering company that happens to make products.**

## 2. Core Messaging Framework

### Primary Brand Statement
> *"We amplify human potential through engineering obsession."*

### Positioning Statement
STIMULAI is a human-performance engineering company operating at the intersection of advanced materials, embedded intelligence, and elite athletics — building machines that don't just respond to athletes, they extend them.

### Messaging Pillars

**Pillar 1 — Engineering Conviction**
We are not fast-followers. We do not iterate on existing paradigms. We question the physics, re-examine the assumptions, and build from first principles. Every decision is engineered, not designed by committee.

*Voice:* Precise. Declarative. Uncompromising.
*Example:* "We don't refine what exists. We replace it."

**Pillar 2 — Human Performance at the Center**
The athlete is not a user. They are a system — biomechanical, psychological, physical — and our machines are engineered to interface with that system at the highest possible fidelity. Performance is not a feature. It is the architecture.

*Voice:* Intimate. Technical. Respectful of the athlete.
*Example:* "Built around the athlete. Not around the sport."

**Pillar 3 — Materials Intelligence**
The choice of material is a statement of intent. We work with materials that most manufacturers consider too difficult, too expensive, or too advanced for the current market. We consider this a feature, not a liability.

*Voice:* Authoritative. Scientific. Premium.
*Example:* "The material is the message."

**Pillar 4 — Embedded Intelligence**
The intelligence in our machines is not an add-on. It is structural. Sensors, algorithms, and adaptive systems are integrated from the initial design brief — not retrofitted at the end of the engineering cycle.

*Voice:* Forward-looking. Confident. Technical.
*Example:* "Intelligence isn't added. It's engineered in."

**Pillar 5 — Future Trajectory**
The bicycle is first. The methodology — human-performance engineering — scales across every sport, every discipline, every interface between human capability and mechanical possibility.

*Voice:* Visionary. Controlled. Strategic.
*Example:* "The bicycle is the beginning."

## 4. Audience Segments

### Segment A — The Elite Athlete
**Profile:** Competitive cyclist, triathlete, or performance-sport athlete. Age 25–45. Deeply technical. Self-directed. Willing to invest significantly in marginal gains. Reads power data, not brochures.

**Motivation:** Performance advantage. The belief that their equipment is not the limit.

**What they need from the website:** Proof. Technical depth. Specificity. Numbers that mean something.

**Emotional state arriving:** Skeptical. They've been sold "the best" too many times by brands that couldn't back it up.

**What the site must do:** Earn credibility through engineering specificity, not through lifestyle imagery.

---

### Segment B — The Performance-Curious Enthusiast
**Profile:** Serious amateur. Age 30–50. Successful professional. Uses sport as a performance outlet. Follows F1, reads Cycling Weekly, tracks power-to-weight ratio. Aspires to elite-level kit.

**Motivation:** Identity. Quality. The feeling of using something genuinely exceptional.

**What they need from the website:** Aspiration with substance. The sense that they are buying into something rare.

**Emotional state arriving:** Excited. Open. Looking for a reason to commit.

**What the site must do:** Make them feel like they've discovered something most people don't know about yet.

---

### Segment C — The Technology Investor / Partner
**Profile:** Venture capital, corporate innovation arm, technology company with adjacent product line. Looking for first-mover opportunities in sports tech, embedded intelligence, or advanced materials.

**Motivation:** ROI. IP positioning. Strategic alignment with a category-defining company.

**What they need from the website:** Conviction. Clarity about the technology platform. Evidence of engineering depth. A clear vision for category expansion.

**Emotional state arriving:** Evaluative. Professional. Looking for signals of serious intent.

**What the site must do:** Communicate that STIMULAI is an engineering company that happens to make a bicycle — not a bicycle company trying to be a tech company.

---

### Segment D — The Engineering Talent
**Profile:** Mechanical engineer, materials scientist, embedded systems developer, or biomechanics researcher. Mid-career. Technically excellent. Bored with their current environment.

**Motivation:** Meaningful problems. Proximity to elite athletes. Cross-disciplinary challenge.

**What they need from the website:** A sense of the intellectual environment. The caliber of the problems they'd be solving. Evidence that this is a company of engineers, not a company with engineers.

**Emotional state arriving:** Intrigued. Cautiously optimistic. Slightly disbelieving.

**What the site must do:** Make them want to apply before they reach the Careers page.

## 7. Emotional Design Goals

### The First 5 Seconds

A visitor lands on the STIMULAI website. They have not scrolled. They have not read a word. In 5 seconds, they must feel:

1. **"This is not what I expected."** — The visual language must immediately subvert the expectation of a bicycle brand. No cycling imagery in the hero. No athlete in a jersey. No product shot. Something that reads as *engineering technology* before it reads as *sports*.

2. **"These people are serious."** — The typography is disciplined. The color palette is severe. The layout is structured. There is no clutter, no decoration without purpose, nothing that doesn't earn its place.

3. **"I need to know more."** — There is something beneath the surface. A depth signal. A technical layer being suggested without being fully revealed. The design rewards curiosity.

**The emotional sequence:**
- Second 1: Orientation — *"What is this?"*
- Second 2: Impact — *"This looks different."*
- Second 3: Interest — *"This feels premium."*
- Second 4: Engagement — *"I want to understand this."*
- Second 5: Commitment — *"I'm going to scroll."*

**Emotional states the site must produce:**
- **Awe** — at the engineering ambition
- **Trust** — through precision and specificity
- **Aspiration** — the sense of being near something exceptional
- **Urgency** — the feeling that this company is moving, and you should pay attention
- **Respect** — earned through intellectual depth, not lifestyle imagery

# PHASE 5 — VISUAL DIRECTION

---

## Art Direction

The visual language of STIMULAI sits between two reference points: **aerospace engineering documentation** and **elite athletic performance photography**.

Neither clinical. Neither lifestyle. Both.

The aesthetic grammar is: **precision surfaces, controlled contrast, structural tension.**

Everything in the visual language communicates that this company operates at the edge of physical possibility — and that it does so with absolute control.

---

## Photography Style

### Engineering Photography
- Macro detail of materials: carbon fiber weave, metal alloy surfaces, sensor integration points
- Architectural lighting: single-source, high-contrast, deep shadow
- No lifestyle context — the material is the subject
- Black or near-black backgrounds exclusively
- Extreme close-ups: the texture of a surface, the geometry of a joint, the precision of a tolerance

### Product Photography
- Product alone, on black surface or floating on black
- Lit from above or from one side — architectural, not commercial
- No human in frame for hero product shots
- 3D render quality preferred — clean, precise, almost unreal
- Where human is included: athlete in performance context, caught in motion, never posed

### Athlete Photography (if used)
- Black and white or extreme high-contrast
- Motion blur or freeze-frame at peak performance
- No jersey logos, no sponsor marks, no cycling-event context
- The athlete as a physical system — body in space, not personality in frame

### Prohibited Photography
- Cyclist in a group on a scenic road
- Smiling athlete holding a product
- Outdoor bright-light lifestyle photography
- Any image that could appear in a cycling magazine

---

## Rendering Style

Where CGI or 3D is used (strongly recommended for hero visuals and product showcase):

- **Material realism over stylization** — metals, carbon, composites rendered with photorealistic surface fidelity
- **Isolated environments** — product in void, no environmental context
- **Technical annotation overlays** — fine lines, measurement markers, section views (used sparingly as interaction elements)
- **Exploded views** — showing component relationships, used in technology section
- **Camera language:** long focal length, shallow depth of field, dramatic framing

---

## Motion Principles

**Principle 1 — Motion has meaning.**
Every animation communicates something. Stagger communicates sequence. Count-up communicates precision. A line drawing in communicates reveal. Motion is not decoration.

**Principle 2 — Restraint over excess.**
One well-executed entrance animation per section. Not every element moves. Not every interaction responds. The moments that do move earn their motion.

**Principle 3 — Duration hierarchy:**
- `150ms` — micro-interactions (hover, focus)
- `300ms` — transitions (button states, card hovers)
- `500ms` — section entrances
- `800ms` — cinematic moments (hero load, product reveal)

**Principle 4 — Easing:**
- `ease-out` for entrances (fast in, decelerate) — feels deliberate
- `ease-in-out` for transitions — feels smooth
- No `linear` animations (feels mechanical, not engineered)
- No `bounce` easing (inappropriate tone)

**Principle 5 — Reduced motion respect:**
All scroll-triggered and entrance animations must have `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` fallbacks. Fade-in only, no translation or scale.
